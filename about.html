Our stance

Why Contraband exists

Because the surest sign a book matters is that someone in power wants it gone.

Across the country — and pointedly in Texas, under the state's book-rating law (HB 900, the "READER Act") and the removals it has driven — the books disappearing from school shelves share a pattern. They tend to be about the parts of American life some would rather not teach: slavery and its afterlife, the Holocaust and internment, the civil-rights movement, and the ordinary struggle of young people to be seen. They are, overwhelmingly, books by and about people of color, and books that ask readers to reckon with hard history.

Contraband collects those books and turns each one into a rigorous, standards-aligned learning room — vocabulary, evidence investigation, analysis, and discussion — so the response to “you can't read this” becomes “let's read it closely, and talk about what it's really saying.”

How we choose titles

Every book here meets three tests:

  • Widely taught. A long-standing part of American grade 6–12 curricula, not a fringe pick.
  • Actually challenged. Documented removals or challenges in real districts, frequently in Texas.
  • Worth the fight. It teaches real history, social justice, or identity with literary substance — the Anne Frank standard.

How the rooms are built

Each learning room uses the same engine as our sister project, PlotPoint: an Enter the Story overview, vocabulary practice, an evidence “Crack the Case” investigation, close-reading analysis, and an extension task — aligned in good faith to Common Core, with TEKS/ELPS flagged for local review.

⚠ Copyright — read this

Most challenged books are still under copyright. Contraband rooms are independent educational companions. They reproduce no text and invent no quotations — every activity is transformative analysis. Students need lawful access to their own copy of the book; we never host, link to, or replace it. Copyrighted rooms are built one at a time against the teacher's own verified text, and each ships with its title-specific companion disclaimer.

What we don't do

No logins, no accounts, no analytics, no data collection. Everything runs in the browser. We take no position on any individual family's choices for their own child — only on the removal of books from everyone's shelf.

Get involved

Teachers: if a book you love has been pulled and you want a room for it, that's the pipeline. Public-domain titles ship fast; copyrighted ones we build against a verified text so the analysis is accurate and quotation-free.
